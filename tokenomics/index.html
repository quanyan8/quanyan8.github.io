About the book

Tokens as infrastructure for intelligence

The token is the kilowatt-hour of a new utility. It measures a flow of computation, it is billed by the million, and it links what an application consumes to the silicon, energy, and capital that produce it.

We draw electricity from the grid, data from wireless networks, and water from a municipal utility, each as a metered service that rests on shared, capital-intensive infrastructure. Machine intelligence is becoming a service of the same kind. As with those earlier infrastructures, its economics is the step that follows its development and the stepping stone to its governance and regulation.

Organizations that adopt AI need this understanding before wide adoption, not after the first surprising bill or the first costly failure. The literature, however, is spread across computer science, economics, engineering, management, and operations research. This book brings these strands together around a single object, the token, and a single organizing chain, from production through consumption to value.

Features

Built to be used, checked, and taught

Every chapter opens with learning objectives and closes with key takeaways, notes on the literature, and exercises. Beyond that common frame, the book is organized around six features.

7 labsAppendix C

Laboratory exercises

Seven labs work through the main results of the chapters, six of them on public agent traces, a leaderboard snapshot, posted and spot prices, and logged runs. They run offline in Python, and 108 automated checks confirm the expected results.

Every numberreproduced by script

Fully computed worked examples

Each worked example is carried through to the final figure, and every number in it is recomputed by a script that accompanies the book, so a reader can change an assumption and follow its effect.

3 systemsrunning examples

Examples that run through the book

A customer-support assistant, an invoice-reconciliation agent calibrated on 480 metered runs, and a multi-agent security-operations workflow are carried from chapter to chapter.

152 figuresmany from primary data

Pictures that explain faster than prose

Cost curves, frontiers, and demand curves sit beside figures redrawn from primary data: agent traces, model leaderboards, market prices, and labor statistics.

63 boxesdated “In Practice”

Structural results, dated facts

Prices and vendor facts appear only in boxes dated to the month in which they were checked. The more than 100 propositions and theorems, with proofs, do not depend on any price list.

210 exercisesand two primers

Ready for a course

Ten exercises per chapter, hints on about half, primers on probability, queueing, and optimization and on economics, a glossary of about 300 terms, and three suggested course paths.

Structure of the book

From production to consumption to value

The book follows one chain, from how silicon, energy, and capital become tokens, through how applications and agents spend them, to what the spending buys. Pricing, markets, governance, and risk are built around this chain, in twenty-one chapters, seven parts, and four appendices.

Appendix C · Laboratory exercises

Seven labs that put the theory to work

Each lab states its objectives and background, sets tasks, prints the expected results, and closes with extensions and a short memo as its deliverable. Six of them run on frozen copies of public data and logged runs, and the seventh on the hypothetical parameters of the insurance chapter. All run offline, so no account with any provider is needed, and the chapters point to the labs that work through their results.

Lab 1

Where an Agent's Tokens Go

Recount the tokens of real agent runs by origin, check the accounting identity of the bill and the quadratic growth of replayed context, regress the meter, write an invoice, and design an audit.

Data: 1,980 τ-bench, 4,448 τ²-bench, and 20,009 SWE-agent traces · Ch. 3, 6, 7 · in the sample

Lab 2

Forecasting and Capping an Agent's Bill

Estimate the heavy tail of run costs, compare reruns of the same task, forecast a bill while the run is under way, set a monthly budget under a cap, and test circuit breakers.

Data: SWE-agent and τ-bench traces, reruns on SWE-bench Verified · Ch. 7, 8, 17

Lab 3

Choosing a Model and an Effort Level

Build the cost frontier of public model configurations, trace effort ladders, meet quality floors with a linear-programming certificate, and fit a router and a cascade on logged runs.

Data: leaderboard snapshot of November 2026, 480 runs of the invoice agent · Ch. 3, 7, 9, 10

Lab 4

Pricing an Agent: Tokens, Tasks, and Outcomes

Compare token, task, and outcome tariffs and their risk loadings, cap a coding agent under token and outcome pay, and solve the informativeness test and an exact contract on logged runs.

Data: τ²-bench conversations, SWE-agent traces, invoice-agent runs · Ch. 13, 14

Lab 5

Buying Inference on a Spot Market

Compute the cost floor of serving on an accelerator, measure price dispersion and the limits of arbitrage, value the option to wait, fit a two-factor price model, and hedge uncertain usage.

Data: posted prices of 1,461 router endpoints, a month of hourly spot prices for 74 models · Ch. 2, 4, 13, 15, 16

Lab 6

Budgets for Agent Teams

Allocate a shared token budget across teams with S-shaped completion curves, randomize caps to close the duality gap, and watch price adjustment cycle.

Data: SWE-agent and τ-bench traces, hypothetical teams · Ch. 11, 17

Lab 7

Insuring an Agent

Thin the stream of agent actions to insured events, price a layer on a spliced severity, study the incentives of a deductible, size the capital of a book, and trace a cascade across three firms.

Parameters: the containment extension of the security-operations example, all hypothetical · Ch. 18

A note on the word. “Tokenomics” was first used in cryptocurrency, for the supply schedules and incentives of digital assets. That usage is unrelated to this book beyond the shared word. Here a token is the unit in which a language model reads and writes, and the unit in which its work is billed.
